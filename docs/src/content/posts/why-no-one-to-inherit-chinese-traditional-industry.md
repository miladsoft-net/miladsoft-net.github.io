> Today, on my way to work by bus, I saw a video pushed by Chongqing TV. A little boy was learning to play the drum for Peking Opera with his father.

When the father has a performance, he sometimes takes the boy with him; the performers sing Peking Opera on stage, and the boy plays the small drum on the left side of the stage, sometimes sitting for two hours. The boy's parents said that he has liked playing the drum since he was a child and is quite good at it, so the troupe often takes him to perform, and sometimes he even goes on stage.

Watching this video, I thought, children's thoughts are really innocent; playing the drum is just something the child is curious about, after all, we can't fathom a child's curiosity and thoughts. But as parents, we should rationally guide the child's interests. Of course, I don't think there's anything wrong with playing the drum, no, I just think playing the drum is not good. In this era, what can playing the drum bring you? As an adult, I don't think about inheriting Chinese traditional culture or anything like that. We should focus on what it can bring us; when your child really engages in this industry, in today's rapidly changing world, can he support himself? Can he support his children? Can he support his aging parents?

This suddenly reminded me of something my parents often said when I was a child: "Can this be a meal ticket?" You really understand the meaning of this sentence when you grow up and feel the pressure. Children do things based on interest, adults should consider gains and losses.

This is probably why so many excellent traditional industries in China have no one willing to inherit them; because most of these traditional industries can't bring us ideal returns, they can only be continued as an extra interest when conditions permit, and are always difficult to become the main business.

In another ten years, traditional industries may no longer exist, but what does it matter! Maybe by then, the city will be overgrown with weeds.
