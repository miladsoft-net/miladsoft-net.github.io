I failed the Zolplay interview, and I am very sad.

Actually, I was confident that I could pass, after all, I am quite confident in my Laravel skills. But unfortunately, I was wrong, and I might be far from my expectations. I started thinking that if I could get into this company, I could happily write Laravel again and learn more in an environment full of experts. But now it seems I need to plan my future path carefully.

The reason I want to change is that I feel I am not learning much in my current job, or rather, the time I put in does not match the rewards I get. I am worried that if this continues, I will lose my competitiveness, so I thought about changing. This short attempt made me realize that my abilities might not be up to my expectations, and I need to calm down and think about how to improve myself.

The first thing I need to do is spend more time learning English. Only by mastering English will I have more options in the future and dare to expand my choices. Currently, I only check in on Duolingo every day, and I use OpenAI for translation during communication. Although I have made some progress over the past year, there has been no qualitative change; my spoken English is still poor, I don't attend English speaking classes, I can't understand what others are saying, and I don't have the courage to participate in English interviews.

The second thing I need to do is to learn deeply from my current job and dig out more valuable content. For example, the lifecycle of a request in the company project, the overall deployment architecture, the middleware used; how to mimic the company's project architecture to build a similar one; the complete business logic of the part I am currently responsible for. From these, I can always learn more things that I previously ignored. Since there are no better changes, I need to maintain the stability of my current job.

The third thing I need to do is to keep my passion, always be enthusiastic about new things, and dare to try. Learn more new technologies and try to create different demos. Output more, even if it's just posting some small opinions and learning insights on Twitter. Also, keep an eye on the companies or projects I like, contribute my PRs even if it's just changing a word. I believe that as long as you put in ten times the effort into a project and submit various PRs or help solve different problems, one day you will be noticed by the project OWNER, and you might receive an interview opportunity. This is also a small selfish motive for participating in open source.

This world is full of people better than you, but as long as you keep being serious, they will lose.
